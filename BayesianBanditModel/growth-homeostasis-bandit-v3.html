Growth homeostasis as a Bayesian bandit

Revision 3 · the model is fitted to every experiment and validated on held-out statistics (section 11), and the cross-lab transfer test has now been run (section 12). Amber marks revision-2 changes.

What 16 experiments, 148,339 cell cycles and 2,237 lineages in this repository say about the variables, their correlations within and across generations, and a hypothesis for the posterior each cell carries.

1 · The growth model and its variables

Each cell grows exponentially during one cycle. Three identities tie seven measured quantities together, so only three are free per generation:

φ = ln(xτ/x₀) = α τhow much it grew
Δ = xτ − x₀ = x₀(eφ − 1)what it added
x₀′ = f · xτ  ⇒  r = f eφ = x₀′/x₀what the daughter inherits

Balanced growth requires ⟨ln r⟩ = 0: on average a daughter is born the size its mother was. The data sit on that manifold: pooled CV of r is 0.17 per cell, but the CV of lineage time-averages of r is only 0.039. Lineages differ in how they get there (slow and big, or fast and small), not in whether they get there.

2 · Coefficient of variation, experiment by experiment

Two robust facts. The division ratio is the most precisely controlled variable in every experiment, roughly three times tighter than anything else. And growth-rate variability is the one quantity whose CV depends on the lab, not only on the medium: Wang and Tanouchi report CV(α) ≈ 0.1, this lab's mother and sister machines ≈ 0.15–0.24.

3 · Intra-generational correlations

  • φ–τ (+0.85) and φ–Δ (+0.73). Fold growth is set by how long the cell waits, and the added length is the visible product of that wait.
  • x₀–φ (−0.39), x₀–Δ (+0.10). Cells born large grow fewer folds but add the same length: the adder. Regression slopes of xτ on x₀ run 0.85–1.35 across experiments, median ≈ 1.1.
  • τ–α (−0.28). Faster growers divide sooner, but only partially: α and φ are essentially uncorrelated (+0.01), so τ absorbs α-fluctuations to protect φ.
  • f is nearly orthogonal to the growth variables (|r| ≤ 0.25) except through x₀ (+0.42), which it mechanically sets.

4 · Inter-generational correlations

Three regimes. Sizes persist: x₀ and xτ have autocorrelation 0.4–0.7, with the adder predicting exactly 0.5 and the Wang MG1655 experiments running above it (slope > 1, lag-3 still 0.39). Growth rate persists weakly: 0.05–0.4, more in this lab's data. Timing anti-persists: φ, τ and r are negatively autocorrelated (≈ −0.1 to −0.2) in every experiment, which is the signature of homeostasis correcting last generation's overshoot. Δ and f are close to memoryless.

5 · Lineages and microenvironments

In the long mother-machine traces (60–200 generations) lineage identity explains almost nothing of φ, τ and f (1–5%) and a modest share of size and α. In the sister machine (25 generations per lineage) the lineage share jumps to 25–40% for τ, x₀ and α. Short traces inside a shared trap do not have time to forget their microenvironment.

The lineage-level structure is almost one-dimensional: ᾱ–τ̄ = -0.92, φ̄–f̄ = -0.89, x̄₀–x̄τ = +0.96. Microenvironments move lineages along a ridge where faster growth buys shorter cycles and smaller division ratios buy larger fold growth, with r̄ pinned at 1 along the whole ridge. That is what "clustered around local maxima" looks like in these data: the clusters are points on the r̄ = 1 manifold, each with its own (ᾱ, Δ̄) coordinate.

6 · Sisters versus neighbours

The sister machine contains two kinds of pair: sister lineages (SL, n = 130 traps) descended from one mother, and neighbour lineages (NL, n = 85) sharing a trap but not a mother. Comparing them separates inheritance from shared environment.

Sisters start strongly synchronised (τ 0.74, x₀ 0.69, φ 0.55) and decay to the neighbour floor within two to three generations. Neighbours sit on that floor from the start (α ≈ 0.3–0.4, τ ≈ 0.2). The floor is the trap; the excess is the mother.

Time-averages tell the longer story. Over 25 generations, sister lineages correlate at α 0.81 and τ 0.86; neighbours at α 0.65 and τ 0.59. Roughly two thirds of the lineage-level sharing is microenvironment and one third is heritable state that survives far longer than the two-generation decay visible per generation. There are two inherited timescales, which is the paper's title.

7 · Hypothesis: the posterior a cell carries

Read the facts above as constraints on a decision process. Each generation the cell commits to a growth rate, a target added length and a division ratio before it can see how the cycle ends. That is a bandit: three arms pulled once per cycle, reward paid in realised growth, with a prior inherited from the mother. The hypothesis is that the cell pulls by Thompson sampling from a Gaussian posterior in log space, and that the posterior mean is a hierarchy of slowly and quickly forgotten estimates of the local environment.

E
Environment (experiment)
Fixed means μα, μΔ, μf = ½. Where the local maximum sits in phenotype space. Fitted per experiment; never shared across experiments.
T
Trap / microenvironment
Offsets eα, eΔ, ef ~ N(0, σenv²), constant for the trap's lifetime, shared by both lineages in it. Sets the neighbour floor.
S
Slow inherited state (posterior mean)
sn = ρslow sn−1 + noise, ρslow ≈ 0.97 (memory ≈ 30 generations). Copied to both daughters at division, then drifts independently. Sets the sister excess in time-averages. Revised: acts mainly on α; the slow Δ component is small, because the long x₀ memory is now explained by the gain k (section 10).
F
Fast inherited state
an = ρfast an−1 + noise, ρfast ≈ 0.4, on α only. Copied to both daughters. Sets lag-1 α persistence and the two-generation sister decay.
P
Pull the arms
ln αn = μα + eα + sα,n + an;   Δn = [Δ̄ eeΔ + sΔ,n + k (x₀ − x̄₀)] · eN(0, σΔ²);   fn = ½ + ef + N(0, σf²). Then physics: xτ = x₀ + Δ, eφ = xτ/x₀, τ = φ/α, x₀′ = f xτ.

Why this and not a flat adder with iid noise: iid noise cannot produce lineage shares of 25–40%, cannot separate sisters from neighbours, and cannot give α an autocorrelation that differs between labs. Why a posterior rather than a fixed trait: the sister excess decays on two timescales, which is what a Bayesian estimate does as observations accumulate (fast forgetting of the last generation's evidence, slow drift of the prior). Why the adder is the "physics" rather than an arm: φ and τ are the only negatively autocorrelated variables, and that sign appears automatically once Δ is the chosen quantity and x₀ is inherited.

The posterior itself, stated in one line: at generation n the cell holds p(ln α, ln Δ, f | history) = N(mn, Σ), with Σ diagonal and fixed (σfast,α ≈ 0.11, σΔ ≈ 0.21, σf ≈ 0.045 in log or absolute units) and mn the sum of the E, T, S and F terms. The within-lineage CVs are the posterior widths; the between-lineage and sister structure are the dynamics of mn.

Second, sizes are shared less between sister lineages than Δ and α are (x₀ 0.39 vs Δ 0.62 in the data), which means the lineage-level component of f is lineage-specific rather than trap-shared, and larger than the model's. Both gaps point to missing state, not to a wrong skeleton.

9 · How to check it against the experiments

  1. Normalise for fitting, de-normalise for testing. Work in ln α, ln Δ, logit f. For fitting, centre each experiment on its own means so the ridge of local maxima is removed and the dynamics of mn are fitted across experiments on common ground. For validation, add the experiment means back and compare in µm and hours; a model that only matches z-scores has not matched the attractor r̄ = 1.
  2. Inputs and outputs, stated once. Inputs per experiment: (μα, μΔ), five variances (σenv for α, Δ, f; σslow for α, Δ), two memories (ρslow, ρfast), three posterior widths (σfast,α, σΔ, σf) and one measurement noise. Outputs: full lineage trajectories of all seven variables, so every statistic in sections 1–6 can be recomputed from the model exactly as it was from the data.
  3. Fit by moments, then by likelihood. Moment-match the 7 CVs, 7 lag-1 autocorrelations and 7 lineage shares per experiment (21 targets, 13 parameters). Then treat (sn, an) as hidden states and fit with a Kalman smoother on ln α and ln Δ; the smoother's posterior is the quantity the hypothesis claims the cell holds, so its innovations should be white.
  4. Three levels, three scorecards. Per experiment: CVs, intra-cell correlation matrix, adder slope. Per lineage: autocorrelation out to lag 5 and the DFA scaling exponent the repository already computes. Pooled: lineage-mean correlation ridge (ᾱ–τ̄ ≈ −0.9, φ̄–f̄ ≈ −0.9) and CV of r̄ ≈ 0.04. A model may use any parameters it likes per experiment; it must not need different ρ values per experiment.
  5. The sister prediction test. Hold out lineage B of every sister pair. Feed the model lineage A's trajectory, run the smoother to get mn at the pair's birth, and predict B's time-averages and its first three generations. Score against the neighbour-pair baseline: the model earns its "sister-predicting" name only if it beats neighbours by the margin the data show (α 0.81 vs 0.65, τ 0.86 vs 0.59). Repeat with NL pairs: predictions must drop to the trap-only level.
  6. Falsifiers. If no shared-at-birth state reproduces τ sister correlation of 0.74 at generation 0, the "three arms" are not the whole decision and a timing arm is needed. If Wang MG1655's lag-3 x₀ autocorrelation (0.39) requires ρslow on Δ that ruins the Tanouchi fit (lag-3 ≈ 0.1), the slow memory is condition-dependent, which breaks the single-posterior story. If the smoother's innovations are autocorrelated, the posterior is not Gaussian or not Markov.
  7. Cross-lab transfer. Fit dynamics on Wang and Tanouchi, refit only (μ, σenv) on this lab's data, and check that the sister-machine scorecard in section 8 is reproduced without touching ρ or the posterior widths. That is the strongest version of "the statistics are easy to understand": the same rule, different environment.

10 · Adding eφ: what changes

eφ = xτ/x₀ = eατ carries no new information per cell (it is a function of variables already in the table), but it is the natural multiplicative variable, and asking the data about it directly changes four things.

Across all 16 experiments ⟨eφ⟩ = 1/⟨f⟩ to within 0.03. Wang's MG1655 mother cells keep 54% of the mother and grow 1.87-fold; Tanouchi's keep 45% and grow 2.2-fold. The local maxima are not at "double your size": each environment sets a division asymmetry and the fold arm is tuned to exactly undo it. The reward the bandit is maximising is r = f eφ = 1, and the fold is the arm it moves to get there.

Size memory is a policy gain, not a hidden state

Regressing added length on birth length within lineages gives a gain k = dΔ/dx₀. It is −0.2 to 0 (adder with a slight sizer lean) in this lab's, Tanouchi's and the lexA3 data, and +0.1 to +0.3 (timer lean) in every Wang MG1655 run. A one-line consequence of x₀′ = f(x₀ + Δ) is acf₁(x₀) = ⟨f⟩(1 + k), and the table shows it holds within 0.03 in 15 of 16 experiments, including the Wang values of 0.6–0.7 that revision 1 attributed to a slow inherited Δ state. Simulating a Wang-like run with k = 0.21 and no slow Δ state gives lag-1/2/3 autocorrelations of 0.63/0.40/0.23 against 0.61/0.46/0.39: the gain explains the first two lags, and only a small slow residual is left for the third. The model gains one input per experiment, k, and loses most of σslow,Δ.

φ is the Gaussian variable; Δ and α are not

Skewness of φ is within ±0.5 in 14 of 16 experiments while eφ is right-skewed at 0.6–2.5, so the posterior over the fold is Gaussian in log space, as assumed. But Δ is right-skewed (0.2–2.5) and ln Δ left-skewed (−0.5 to −4.7) at the same time, with excess kurtosis of 2–17 after removing the x₀ dependence; and ln α has a long lower tail (skew −2 to −17 in the long-trace experiments). Neither a Gaussian nor a log-normal posterior over Δ is right. A heavy-tailed draw (Student-t on ln Δ with 3–5 degrees of freedom, or a mixture with a rare stall component for α) is the honest choice, and the Kalman smoother in step 3 of section 9 should be a robust one.

The fold is an output, not an inherited trait

Compensation: Var(φ) is 0.15–0.27 of Var(ln τ) + Var(ln α) in every experiment outside Tanouchi (0.41–0.47 there). The cell sets τ knowing its α, so three quarters of the timing noise never reaches the fold. And eφ has no heritable slow component: its lineage share of variance is 1–5%, and sister lineages' time-averages correlate at 0.30 against 0.39 for neighbours, i.e. nothing beyond the trap. Sisters share eφ at birth (0.54) only through the mother's size and timing, and that is gone by generation 2. Inheritance lives in α and in the Δ intercept; the fold is what the feedback produces from them.

Revised posterior, in one line

p(ln α, Δ, f | history) = N(mn, Σ) with heavy tails on Δ and ln α;
policy: Δn = Δ̄n + k (x₀ − x̄₀),   target: ⟨eφ⟩ = 1/⟨f⟩k, ⟨f⟩ per experiment

So, yes: adding eφ changes the understanding in two ways that matter for fitting. The slow timescale is almost entirely a growth-rate (α) phenomenon, not a size phenomenon; and the environment-specific part of the policy is a pair (⟨f⟩, k), both measurable directly from the data before any fitting. Validation step 4 gains two checks that cost nothing: ⟨eφ⟩⟨f⟩ = 1 and acf₁(x₀) = ⟨f⟩(1 + k), in physical units, per experiment.

11 · Fitting the model and validating it

What the code is

Plain Python: NumPy for a vectorised simulator of the hierarchy in section 7 (all lineages of an experiment advanced one generation at a time), pandas for the data, SciPy for Student-t draws and Nelder–Mead. No probabilistic-programming library; the likelihood of this model is not tractable because of the deterministic adder map, so the fit is simulation-based: the simulator is run with fixed random seeds and its summary statistics are driven onto the data's. The script is attached alongside this page.

Inputs, outputs, and what is held out

Measured directly, per experiment, not fitted: μα, Δ̄, ⟨f⟩ and the policy gain k. Fitted, per experiment, 15 parameters: trap and lineage variances of α, Δ and f; the two memories ρslow and ρfast (plus a short memory on f); the posterior widths σfast,α, σΔ, σf; Student-t degrees of freedom νΔ, να for the heavy tails; measurement noise. Fitting targets, all scale-free: 8 CVs, lineage-centred correlations between all variable pairs, lag-1 and lag-2 autocorrelations, lineage variance shares, the five z-score quantiles of every variable (5%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 95%, after log for sizes, times and rates), and sister time-average correlations where pairs exist. Held out, never seen by the optimiser: lag-3 autocorrelations, nine mother→daughter correlations, the adder slope, the CV of lineage-mean r, sister correlations at birth, and all physical-unit means. Validation uses a fresh random seed, so it also tests for over-fitting to simulation noise.

What the fit says

  • The posterior is heavy-tailed, and consistently so. νΔ comes out at 3.5–7.5 in every experiment (median 4.3), far from Gaussian; να is 4–7 in most. This is the single biggest change from the hand-set model.
  • The fast memory is a near-constant of the system: ρfast = 0.12–0.61 with no dependence on lab or temperature, and σΔ = 0.16–0.36. These are candidates for the "same rule" in the cross-lab transfer test (step 7 of section 9).
  • Physical units survive de-normalisation. Mean x₀, τ, Δ and α are reproduced within 5% in 14 of 16 experiments, and the CV of lineage means within 0.03 in most.
  • The slow memory is not identified from a single long lineage. ρslow trades off against the lineage variance of α in the mother-machine runs; only the sister-machine data, which has pairs, pins it. It needs the sister test to be fitted properly.
  • Failures, named. Wang 0512 (CV Δ 0.5, KS 0.24) is not reproduced; its data has a long-cell tail the adder cannot produce. In all Wang MG1655 runs the mother's final length and the daughter's birth length correlate at only 0.80 in the data against 0.96 in the model, which says the two lengths are measured by independent regressions with noise larger than the model's σmeas; that is a measurement model, not biology. Sister synchrony at birth stays under-predicted (0.24 vs 0.48 for τ), the missing timing state from section 8.

So the model, as fitted, reproduces the correlations and CVs per experiment, per lineage and pooled, in normalised units and in physical units, with one environment-specific pair (⟨f⟩, k) and a set of noise widths that barely move between labs. What it does not yet contain is a shared division-timing state and a proper measurement model for the older datasets.

12 · The cross-lab transfer test

Section 9 called this the strongest check and section 11 admitted it had not been run. It has now. The 15 parameters are split in two: a rule (the per-generation posterior widths σfast,α, σΔ, σf, the heavy-tail exponents νΔ, να, and the short memories ρfast, ρf) and an environment structure (trap and lineage variances, ρslow, measurement noise). The rule is frozen at the median of the fits from the training labs; on each test experiment only the six structure parameters are refitted, and the measured inputs (μα, Δ̄, ⟨f⟩, k) come from the test data as always. Scored on the same held-out statistics as section 11, with a fresh seed.
