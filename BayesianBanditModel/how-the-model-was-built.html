How the bacterial growth model was built, fitted and tested

A short, complete account of what was done: the data, the model's equations, the statistics used to compare them, how the parameters were fitted in normalised units, and how the fit was checked on things it had never seen.

1. The data

Single E. coli cells were filmed in microfluidic channels. Each time a cell is born, grows and divides, that is one cycle. The repository contains 148,339 cycles from 2,237 lineages (a lineage is one cell followed through its descendants, always keeping the same pole) across 16 experiments from three labs. In the "sister machine" experiments two lineages share a channel; some pairs are sisters (same mother), some are just neighbours.

Within one cycle the log of the cell's length grows in a straight line. Fitting that line gives everything else:

x₀ = length at birth,   xτ = length at division,   τ = time between them
α = slope of ln(length) vs time growth rate
φ = ln(xτ/x₀) = ατ fold growth  ·  eφ = xτ/x₀ fold ratio
Δ = xτ − x₀ added length  ·  f = x₀ / (mother's xτ) division ratio

Only three of these are free each generation (say α, Δ and f); the rest follow by arithmetic. A model therefore only has to say how α, Δ and f are chosen.

2. The statistics being explained

Everything the model is asked to reproduce is one of these five scale-free quantities, each computed per experiment. Below, x is a variable (say Δ), xn its value in generation n, and ⟨·⟩ an average over all cycles.

nameformulawhat it measures
coefficient of variationCV = σx / ⟨x⟩how noisy x is, in units of its mean
correlationrxy = cov(x, y) / (σx σy)whether two variables of the same cell move together (−1 to 1)
autocorrelation at lag krk = corr(xn, xn+k)memory: does a cell resemble its k-th descendant?
lineage variance share1 − Var(x − x̄lineage) / Var(x)how much of the spread is between lineages rather than within them
z-score quantilesz = (ln x − mean) / sd, then the 5, 25, 50, 75, 95% pointsthe shape of the distribution, not just its width

Correlations and autocorrelations are computed after subtracting each lineage's own mean, so that slow differences between lineages do not masquerade as cell-to-cell structure. Sizes, times and rates are logged first because their noise is multiplicative.

3. The model

The idea is a bandit: each generation the cell "pulls three arms", committing to a growth rate, an amount to add, and a division ratio, before it knows how the cycle will turn out. It pulls them by Thompson sampling, meaning it draws each value at random from its current belief (a probability distribution) about what works in this environment. That belief is inherited from the mother and drifts slowly. Written out:

Layer 1 · the belief, and how it is inherited

sn = ρslow sn−1 + εs slow state, ρslow ≈ 0.9–1, copied to both daughters
an = ρfast an−1 + εa fast state on α, ρfast ≈ 0.3–0.6
e = constant offset per channel the microenvironment, shared by both lineages in it

These are AR(1) processes: each generation keeps a fraction ρ of the previous value and adds fresh noise ε. ρ close to 1 means long memory. Sisters start with the same s and a; neighbours start with the same e only.

Layer 2 · pulling the arms

ln αn = ln μα + eα + sα,n + an
Δn = [ Δ̄ eeΔ + sΔ,n + k (x₀ − x̄₀) ] · eσΔ tν tν = Student-t noise, ν ≈ 4
fn = ⟨f⟩ + ef + (small AR(1) noise)

k is the policy gain: with k = 0 the cell adds a fixed length regardless of birth size (an "adder"); k < 0 leans towards a size target, k > 0 towards a time target. The Student-t gives heavier tails than a Gaussian; ν = ∞ would recover the Gaussian.

Layer 3 · the arithmetic (no free parameters)

xτ = x₀ + Δn,   φn = ln(xτ/x₀),   τn = φn/αn,   x₀ (daughter) = fn xτ
measured lengths = true lengths × eN(0, σmeas²)

Two consequences of layer 3 can be checked by hand and both hold in all 16 experiments: ⟨eφ⟩ = 1/⟨f⟩ (a cell grows exactly enough to undo the asymmetry of its division) and r₁(x₀) = ⟨f⟩(1 + k) (the lag-1 memory of birth size).

4. Normalising before fitting, de-normalising after

Each experiment sits at a different point in phenotype space (different medium, temperature, strain): cells are 1.6 µm in one and 3.8 µm in another. Fitting the model to raw micrometres would spend all its effort matching those offsets. So:

  1. Measured, not fitted. The experiment's means μα, Δ̄, ⟨f⟩ and the gain k (a regression slope of Δ on x₀ within lineages) are read directly from the data and plugged in.
  2. Fitted on scale-free statistics only. Every fitting target in section 2 is dimensionless, and the quantiles are taken in z-units (log, then standardised by the data's own mean and standard deviation). This puts all experiments on one footing.
  3. Validated back in physical units. After fitting, the model's output in µm and hours is compared with the data's mean x₀, τ, Δ and α. A model that only matched z-scores could still be the wrong size; this check catches that.

5. How the parameters were fitted

Fifteen parameters per experiment: the variances of e, s and a; ρslow, ρfast; σΔ, σf; the two Student-t ν's; measurement noise; and three minor ones. The likelihood of this model is intractable (layer 3 is a deterministic map), so the fit is simulation-based:

L(θ) = Σi wi ( ŝi − si(θ) )² ŝi: statistic from data, si(θ): same statistic from a simulation with parameters θ
  • The simulator advances every lineage of the experiment one generation at a time (vectorised with NumPy), using the real number of lineages and generations.
  • It is run with a fixed random seed during fitting ("common random numbers"), so L(θ) is a smooth function of θ and not swamped by simulation noise.
  • About 90 targets per experiment: 8 CVs (weight 3), 28 pairwise correlations, 16 autocorrelations at lags 1–2, 8 lineage shares, 40 z-quantiles (weight 0.5), plus sister correlations where pairs exist.
  • L is minimised with Nelder–Mead, a derivative-free simplex search, 1,800 evaluations per experiment, parameters clipped to sensible ranges (e.g. 0.5 ≤ ρslow ≤ 0.999, ν ≥ 2.2).

Why not a Bayesian sampler (Stan, PyMC)? They need a likelihood. The honest next step would be a Kalman or particle smoother over the hidden states s and a, which also gives the "posterior the cell holds" directly.

6. Validation: things the optimiser never saw

After fitting, the model is re-run with a new seed and compared on statistics that were deliberately left out of L:

  • autocorrelations at lag 3 (only lags 1–2 were fitted),
  • nine mother→daughter correlations (e.g. mother's xτ vs daughter's φ),
  • the regression slope of xτ on x₀ (the "adder slope"),
  • the CV of each lineage's mean of r = f eφ, which should be tiny if growth is balanced,
  • sister correlations at the first generation,
  • and, after de-normalising, the physical-unit means.

7. What came out, and what did not

  • Heavy tails are real. The fitted ν for Δ is 3.5–7.5 in every experiment (a Gaussian would be ν → ∞). The cell's "belief" about how much to add has fat tails.
  • Two parameters are nearly universal: ρfast ≈ 0.3–0.6 and σΔ ≈ 0.2–0.3, regardless of lab, temperature or strain. The environment-specific part collapses to (⟨f⟩, k).
  • Size memory is the gain, not an inherited state. Experiments with k ≈ +0.2 have long size memory; those with k ≈ −0.15 do not. No hidden slow size variable is needed.
  • Three things it gets wrong. Sisters' first-generation timing is more synchronised than the model predicts (data 0.48, model 0.24): a shared division-timing state is missing. In the oldest datasets the mother's final length and daughter's birth length correlate at 0.80 vs the model's 0.96, which points to measurement noise that the model's σmeas is too simple to capture. And one experiment with a long tail of filamentous cells is not reproduced at all.
  • One parameter is poorly identified. ρslow cannot be separated from the lineage variance of α using single long lineages; it needs paired (sister) data.

8. The whole pipeline in one picture

Code: a single Python script (NumPy, pandas, SciPy); no modelling library. Data: the repository's processed files with lineage-specific 3σ outliers removed. All numbers quoted here were computed in the same session as the fits.
